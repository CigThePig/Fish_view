## How one tap currently lands

Seeds 5, 147 and 1234, each a 420-day aquarium settled for a further minute,
then one tap at mid-depth in open water.

Before the tap the fifteen persistent fish are spread over **six to eight
different activities**. One tick after it, all fifteen are in `touch-react` —
**one activity, whole cast, every seed**. The school is steered at the same
point, the nearest fish takes a small permanent boldness and sociability drift,
and a single `state.reaction` runs for 3.2 seconds. Pointer movement, hold and
release carry no meaning at all.

That collapse is the thing Stage 2 exists to end, and it is the measurement
Phase 2's gate has to move: after Phase 2 the same tap must leave several
distinct activities standing while still acknowledging the touch immediately.

## Renderer headroom

Mature aquarium, 245–252 scene objects, 1 102–1 172 glyphs.

| | average | worst | rectangles/frame | full redraws |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 30 s untouched | 54.0 % | 98.1 % | 15.6–20.0 | 0 |
| 4 s after one tap | 54.8 % | 95.6 % | — | 0 |

Read that carefully before adding anything that paints. The incremental
renderer never falls back to a full redraw, but a mature aquarium already
repaints **about half the panel every frame**, and single frames come within a
couple of percent of the whole screen. Stage 2's environmental effects, surface
response and interaction aftermath are being added on top of that, on a device
that has to hold 8–10 FPS. The headroom is thin; a feature that raises the
average materially, or that starts changing the background signature, is a
Phase 10 failure no matter how it looks in a browser.

The showcase lab's per-activity damage (`npm run measure:readability`) sits
lower, 35–43 % average, because the lab scene is sparser than a stocked tank.
Compare like with like.

## Save size

One aquarium, fifty taps applied at each age, serialised through
`serializePersistentState`.

| Age | Fish | Plants | Bytes |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 30 days | 3 | 22 | 9 476 |
| 1 year | 15 | 28 | 17 166 |
| 3 years | 15 | 30 | 17 843 |
| 10 years | 15 | 30 | 17 853 |

The save saturates: a decade costs ten more bytes than three years, and taps
cost nothing, because the persisted record is biology and history rather than a
log of what the aquarium has been through. `persistenceVersion` is 2 and the
payload carries no transient interaction key.

Phase 6 adds a per-fish relationship measure. It must keep this shape: a
compact bounded scalar per fish, no interaction history, and a stocked ten-year
save still comfortably under 24 KB — the ceiling
`tests/stage-2-invariants.test.js` now guards.

Two pairs to watch, because Phase 7 has to pull them apart:

- **individual-follow and playful-chase** differ mainly in speed (0.56 against

  40°/s turn rate is the highest in the table — higher than the chase — which is
  the opposite of the relaxed matched pair it is meant to read as.

## What is already in place for Stage 2

Worth knowing before building, because the plan says to evolve this rather than
replace it:

- plant specimens already bend near touch and near persistent fish, though the
  disturbance ignores vertical distance
- a substrate touch already releases a small deterministic bubble burst
- persistent fish already physically disturb bubbles
- the renderer already draws the expanding reaction ripple
- touch-created bubbles are already marked investigable — but no fish can
  investigate one, because the global `touch-react` outlives them. Phase 5 has
  to make that path genuinely reachable.
- the water surface is autonomous and does not react to touch at all
- snails, shrimp, drifting tufts and habitat structures do not react to the
  viewer at all
